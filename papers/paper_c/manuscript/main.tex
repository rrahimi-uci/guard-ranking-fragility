\title{What Does Reference Centering Buy a Safety Guard?\\
\large A Matched SFT, PairCE, and DPO Study}
\author{Anonymous authors}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is increasingly used to train language-model
safety guards, but a hard-label binary guard contains no preference information
beyond its class label. In the one-token setting DPO reduces exactly to a
reference-centered signed-margin loss, so the two objectives differ only by a
per-example gradient weight. We show that at initialization this weight ratio is
near-constant on a real guard panel --- mean $1.30$ with coefficient of variation
$0.17$ --- which means reference centering starts life as a larger effective
learning rate rather than as a differential re-emphasis. Any comparison at fixed
step count is therefore confounded, and a single-checkpoint contrast on a
four-checkpoint panel is additionally underpowered: the measured seed standard
deviation of $0.036$ puts its smallest detectable effect at $0.035$, above the
effect an explicit KL anchor achieves on the same suite.

We turn that confound into the hypothesis. Each factorial cell contributes a
represented/transfer trajectory over a saved checkpoint ladder, and the primary
estimand is the transfer gap between objectives \emph{at matched represented
average precision}. A pure learning-rate rescale slides a run along its own
frontier and scores exactly zero; only a genuine anti-forgetting mechanism buys
transfer at equal represented ranking. The design compares VerdictCE, PairCE, and
DPO under uncertain and source/label-matched random selection across four compact
instruction models and five seeds, marginalizing equally across selectors,
bootstrapping over checkpoints rather than cells, and gating the full panel on a
pilot that \emph{measures} the variance reduction the estimand needs. This
design-stage paper contributes the algebra, the rescale lemma and its two design
consequences, preregistered frontier estimands with an executable power gate,
layered artifact locks, and a self-contained execution scaffold. It reports no
performance result yet.
\end{abstract}

\section{Reference centering as a controlled factor}
\label{sec:factorization}

For prompt $x$, let $z_\theta(v\mid x)$ denote the next-token logit for verdict
$v\in\{\mathrm{safe},\mathrm{unsafe}\}$. Define
\begin{align}
  s_\theta(x) &= z_\theta(\mathrm{unsafe}\mid x)
                -z_\theta(\mathrm{safe}\mid x),\\
  y(x) &= \begin{cases}+1,&\text{unsafe},\\-1,&\text{safe},\end{cases}\\
  m_\theta(x) &= y(x)s_\theta(x).
\end{align}
Larger $m_\theta$ always favors the correct verdict.

VerdictCE applies cross-entropy over the complete vocabulary:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{VCE}}
  =-\log\operatorname{softmax}(z_\theta)_{v^+}.
\end{equation}
PairCE discards logits outside the two verdicts and applies a logistic margin
loss,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Pair}}
  =\operatorname{softplus}(-\beta m_\theta).
\end{equation}

For DPO, chosen $v^+$ is the correct verdict and rejected $v^-$ is the other
verdict. Because the normalization term cancels in the chosen--rejected log
probability difference,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DPO}}
  &= -\log\sigma\left(\beta\left[
    \log\frac{\pi_\theta(v^+\mid x)}{\pi_\theta(v^-\mid x)}
   -\log\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(v^+\mid x)}{\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}(v^-\mid x)}
  \right]\right)\\
  &=\operatorname{softplus}\left[-\beta
    (m_\theta-m_{\mathrm{ref}})\right].
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Thus PairCE versus DPO is a controlled uncentered-versus-reference-centered
comparison when $\beta$ is shared. At initialization
$m_\theta=m_{\mathrm{ref}}$, so every DPO example has loss $\log 2$. The
reference is not new preference information, and $\beta$ is not itself evidence
of an achieved KL constraint.

\subsection{What centering does to the gradient, and why that is a threat}

Both margin objectives share a gradient direction and differ only in a scalar
per-example weight:
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}
= -\beta\,w(x)\,\nabla_\theta m_\theta(x),
\qquad
w_{\mathrm{PairCE}}=\sigma(-\beta m_\theta),
\quad
w_{\mathrm{DPO}}=\sigma\!\left(-\beta\left[m_\theta-m_{\mathrm{ref}}\right]\right).
\end{equation}
Centering cannot change the direction of any per-example update. It can only
change how much each example counts.

\begin{lemma}[Centering is a near-uniform rescale at initialization]
\label{lem:rescale}
At $\theta=\theta_{\mathrm{ref}}$ we have $w_{\mathrm{DPO}}\equiv\tfrac12$ for
every example, while $w_{\mathrm{PairCE}}=\sigma(-\beta m_{\mathrm{ref}})$. The
per-example ratio is therefore
$\tfrac12\,\sigma(-\beta m_{\mathrm{ref}})^{-1}$, which is constant in $x$ to the
extent that the reference-margin distribution is narrow. On the Stage-1 margins of
this panel with $\beta=0.1$: $93\%$ of rows have $m_{\mathrm{ref}}>0$, the PairCE
weights have interquartile range $0.05$ about a mean of $0.385$
(coefficient of variation $0.17$), and the mean ratio is $1.30$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[The confound]
At initialization, DPO is approximately PairCE at a $1.3\times$ larger effective
learning rate rather than a differentially re-weighted objective. Any contrast at
fixed step count therefore mixes ``better objective'' with ``faster training,''
and cannot identify the first. Differential re-weighting appears only later, once
$m_\theta$ has moved away from $m_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and rows that improved past the
reference are down-weighted --- a self-limiting behaviour PairCE lacks.
\end{corollary}

Two design consequences follow, and they are the substance of
\Cref{sec:estimands}. First, the primary estimand must be invariant to a
monotone reparameterization of training speed, which the frontier form of
\Cref{eq:frontier} is and a fixed-step AP difference is not. Second, the
checkpoint ladder is not a convenience for selection but the instrument that
separates the early rescale-like regime from the late self-limiting one; a
single saved checkpoint per cell would discard exactly the contrast of interest.
\verb|paper_c effective-lr| reports the quantities in \Cref{lem:rescale} from the
locked reference margins, so the magnitude of the confound is measured on this
panel rather than assumed.

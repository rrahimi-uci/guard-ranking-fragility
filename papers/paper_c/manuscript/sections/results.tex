Two consequences are fixed in advance. First, the single-checkpoint contrast this
study is named after cannot detect its own target effect on this panel, which is
why it is secondary. Second, the frontier estimand clears the target only if
pairing removes at least $67\%$ of the raw variance; the pilot measures that
factor, and the full panel is not authorised until it does.

\subsection{Reporting order}

The completed paper will report evidence in the following fail-closed order:
(1) execution inventory and invalid/infeasible cells; (2) the measured
effective-learning-rate ratio of \Cref{lem:rescale} on the locked reference
margins, which calibrates how much of any fixed-step difference is speed rather
than objective; (3) primary $F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ on represented and transfer, with
$F_{\mathrm{pair}}$ and $F_{\mathrm{total}}$; (4) the secondary point contrasts
$C_{\mathrm{pair}}$, $C_{\mathrm{ref}}$, $C_{\mathrm{total}}$, labelled
confound-exposed; (5) the objective-by-selection interaction; (6) calibration and
stress outcomes; (7) movement and compute diagnostics; and (8) leave-one-source
and leave-one-model sensitivity. If the target-matched panel is incomplete, the
primary aggregate is labelled unavailable rather than silently computed on
survivors.

\subsection{How a null will be reported}

An $F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ interval containing zero is the outcome the mechanism
predicts: centering cannot change the direction of any per-example update, only
its weight. Such a result will be reported as a \emph{bounded null} --- point
estimate, interval, and minimum detectable effect together --- and stated as
``reference centering buys no transfer at equal represented ranking, to within
$\pm\varepsilon$ on this panel,'' never as an absence of evidence. That claim is
useful precisely because DPO is already deployed in guard pipelines on the
assumption that it does something a margin loss does not.

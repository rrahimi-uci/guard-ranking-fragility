\section{Estimands and decision rules}
\label{sec:estimands}

\subsection{Why the obvious estimand will not do}

Let $M_R(o,s;b,r)$ be source-macro average precision in regime $R$ for objective
$o$, selector $s$, model $b$, and seed $r$. The natural contrasts are
\begin{align}
C_{\mathrm{pair}}(R,s)&=M_R(\mathrm{PairCE},s)-M_R(\mathrm{VerdictCE},s),\\
C_{\mathrm{ref}}(R,s)&=M_R(\mathrm{DPO},s)-M_R(\mathrm{PairCE},s),\\
C_{\mathrm{total}}(R,s)&=M_R(\mathrm{DPO},s)-M_R(\mathrm{VerdictCE},s).
\end{align}
$C_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the quantity this study is named after, and we
\emph{demote it to secondary} for two measured reasons.

\paragraph{It is confounded with effective learning rate.}
By \Cref{lem:rescale}, reference centering multiplies each example's gradient
weight by $\tfrac{1}{2}\,\sigma(-\beta m_{\mathrm{ref}})^{-1}$ at initialization.
On this panel's Stage-1 margins that factor has mean $1.30$ with a weight
coefficient of variation of only $0.17$, and $93\%$ of rows sit at positive
margin. DPO therefore begins as PairCE at an approximately $1.3\times$ larger
\emph{effective} learning rate. Because Stage 2 holds the nominal learning rate
fixed across cells, a contrast evaluated at a \emph{fixed step count} cannot
separate ``centering is a better objective'' from ``centering is the same
objective trained faster.'' That is the confound class this design was rebuilt to
remove, and a single-checkpoint $C_{\mathrm{ref}}$ does not remove it.

\paragraph{It is underpowered.}
The within-checkpoint seed standard deviation of transfer macro-AP on the parent
runs is $\sigma=0.0355$. Seeds inside a checkpoint share the model, the frozen
manifest, the recipe, and the data order, so the independent unit for a panel mean
is the checkpoint, not the cell: four units, not twenty. A one-sided $97.5\%$
bound then needs a true effect above $0.035$ before it clears zero --- larger than
the $+0.061$ an \emph{explicit} KL anchor produced on the same suite, and far
larger than a $1.3\times$ gradient rescale should be expected to buy. The original
design could not have distinguished a null from a real small effect.

\subsection{The frontier estimand}

Both problems share one fix. A pure learning-rate rescale moves a run
\emph{along} its own represented/transfer trade-off curve; it does not move the
curve. So we compare curves at matched represented ranking instead of points at
matched step count.

For each cell $(b,r,s,o)$ the checkpoint ladder yields a trajectory
$T_{b,r,s,o}=\{(M_{\mathrm{rep}},M_{\mathrm{tr}})\}$ over saved steps, ordered by
step and reduced to its running maximum in represented AP, so each represented
level is credited to the earliest checkpoint that reached it. For two objectives
sharing $(b,r,s)$ we interpolate both trajectories onto a fixed grid
$\mathcal{G}$ of $21$ represented-AP levels inside their overlapping range and
average the vertical gap:
\begin{equation}
F_{\mathrm{ref}}(b,r,s)=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{G}|}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}
\Big[T^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{DPO}}(g)-T^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\mathrm{PairCE}}(g)\Big],
\label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
where $T^{\mathrm{tr}}(g)$ is transfer AP at represented level $g$. Cells
overlapping by less than $0.01$ represented AP are reported as
\texttt{insufficient\_overlap} rather than extrapolated. $F_{\mathrm{pair}}$ and
$F_{\mathrm{total}}$ are defined identically on their own objective pairs.

\Cref{eq:frontier} has the property the study needs: it is \emph{exactly zero}
when two objectives trace the same frontier at different speeds, and nonzero only
when one buys transfer at equal represented ranking. The confound becomes the null
hypothesis. It also multiplies evidence per GPU-hour --- each cell contributes a
curve rather than a point --- and the exact pairing (same model, seed, selector,
initialization, and scored rows) cancels the checkpoint-level variance component
that dominates $\sigma$ above.

\subsection{Aggregation, intervals, and the power gate}

$F$ is marginalized with equal weight across the two fixed selectors, then across
models, so no selector can be chosen after seeing results and no checkpoint can
dominate the panel. Intervals come from a bootstrap that resamples \emph{models},
not cells; resampling cells would understate the interval by roughly $\sqrt{5}$.

The design is powered only if pairing removes enough variance. With four
clusters, $\sigma=0.0355$, and a target effect of $0.02$ transfer AP, pairing must
remove at least $67\%$ of the raw variance. The pilot of \Cref{sec:design} exists
to \emph{measure} that factor rather than assume it. Until it does, the assumed
value gates the full panel: \verb|paper_c power --gate| exits nonzero whenever the
primary minimum detectable effect exceeds the target, so an underpowered design
cannot be discovered after the compute is spent.

Retrospective results receive paired two-sided $95\%$ intervals. A later
prospective success claim requires simultaneous one-sided $97.5\%$ bounds showing
positive transfer $F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and represented non-inferiority within
$-0.02$, plus absolute harm margins of $0.02$ for safe false positives and
harmful recall. An incomplete or infeasible primary panel cannot pass. An
$F_{\mathrm{ref}}$ interval containing zero is reported as a \emph{bounded null}
alongside its minimum detectable effect --- not as an absence of evidence.

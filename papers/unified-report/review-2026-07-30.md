# Deep reviewer report: *The Safety-Guard Benchmark Chooses the Winner*

**Artifact reviewed:** `unified_report.pdf`, 86 pages  
**Source commit:** `158bc30f424de14054e5c97640f95ceb9d171fb3`  
**Review date:** 2026-07-30  
**Recommendation:** **Major revision; reject in its current claim-bearing form.**

## Executive verdict

There is a useful empirical paper inside this report. Its strongest contribution is the paired design:
compare every tuned guard with its own base on identical rows, separate sources represented during
training from source-held-out transfer, and inspect both ranking and a matched false-alarm budget. That
design is more informative than an ordinary leaderboard. The paper is also unusually candid about many
limitations, including the inspected fixed panel, the mortgage label tier, the empty G1/D0 cell, the
invalid Llama-Guard cell, and the failure of the protected-pair instrument.

The current manuscript is nevertheless not reliable enough for submission. Four blockers affect its
headline evidence rather than its polish:

1. The reproducibility headline is false for the current tree. The paper says 12 of 24 generated inputs
   byte-check; the report now imports 31 generated TeX artifacts, and `reproduce.py --check` crashes after
   10 byte-identical results, 4 environment-gated results, 17 uncovered artifacts, and 3 import errors.
2. The strongest local-versus-frontier headline combines incompatible estimators. The table reports a
   mean of five per-seed metrics, while the paired delta first averages five score vectors, thereby
   evaluating a five-model ensemble and ignoring training-seed uncertainty. This is why the displayed
   arithmetic does not close: `.948 - .741 = .207`, not the reported `+.185`. The code then selects the
   largest significant result from 12 represented-source SFT cells without multiplicity correction.
3. The adaptation study is repeatedly called “preregistered confirmatory,” despite a `dev_nonfinal`
   registry, no release lock, reuse of inspected data, an invalid harness cell retained contrary to its
   eligibility rule, and an omitted preregistered interaction. It is an analysis-preregistered diagnostic,
   not a clean confirmatory study.
4. The mortgage release is neither fully sealed nor pair-isolated as claimed. Its 95-row `private_test`
   is tracked in the public repository, and one true minimal pair is split across train and public test.

These defects do not automatically invalidate the central retrospective Act I observation. They do
invalidate the current confirmatory, frontier-headline, benchmark-integrity, and reproducibility claims.
The right publication decision is therefore major revision, or reject if no revision cycle is available.

## Critical findings

### C1. The reproducibility contribution is currently false and the check crashes

**Severity:** critical  
**Affected dimensions:** correctness, novelty, readability

The abstract says one command byte-checks 12 of 24 generated artifacts
([`unified_report.tex:140-145`](unified_report.tex#L140-L145)); the introduction repeats the tooling as a
novel contribution ([`unified_report.tex:242-245`](unified_report.tex#L242-L245)); and the reproducibility
section says every table and figure is a locked `\input`, that `make reproduce` asserts byte identity,
and that 12/24 artifacts are covered ([`unified_report.tex:822-847`](unified_report.tex#L822-L847)).

All four parts are wrong in the current tree:

- The manuscript has **31** distinct `generated/*.tex` inputs, not 24.
- `make reproduce` calls `reproduce.py` without `--check`; only `make reproduce-check` requests comparison
  ([`Makefile:9-13`](Makefile#L9-L13)). Without `--check`, `_copy_into_generated` overwrites the committed
  copy and labels it “regenerated” ([`reproduce.py:39-51`](reproduce.py#L39-L51)).
- `reproduce.py --check` fails to import `guard_research` in the ExpGuard, frontier, and SFT+SFT paths,
  and the current coverage accounting double-counts failed paths as uncovered. It ends with
  `AssertionError: 10+4+17+3 != 31` at
  [`reproduce.py:457-494`](reproduce.py#L457-L494).
- The figures path always regenerates figures and never compares them, even in check mode
  ([`reproduce.py:385-387`](reproduce.py#L385-L387)). Thus “every table and figure ... asserts
  byte-identity” is not true.
- The check is not side-effect-free: the mortgage re-evaluation rewrote four report files to the fallback
  metric backend in this environment. Those review-induced changes were restored, but a verifier should
  not need to repair the checkout after a check.

**Recommendation.** Make a manifest of every claim-bearing artifact with exactly one state:
`verified`, `environment-gated`, `uncovered`, or `failed`. Have `reproduce-check` work in a temporary
directory, compare tables *and figures*, preserve subprocess failures, and leave the worktree byte-clean.
Then derive the coverage numbers printed in the paper from the manifest itself. Until this passes in a
clean supported environment, remove the abstract and contribution-level reproducibility headline.

### C2. The selected frontier head-to-head compares a table mean with a five-model ensemble

**Severity:** critical  
**Affected dimensions:** correctness, novelty

For an SFT cell, the table follows the Paper A convention: compute TPR/AP/AUROC for each training seed,
then average the five metrics ([`eval_frontier_general_h2h.py:119-132`](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L119-L132)).
But for the paired delta, the code averages the five *score vectors* first and then computes one metric
([`eval_frontier_general_h2h.py:269-286`](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L269-L286)). That
second object is a five-run score ensemble, not the expected performance of one trained guard.

The generated macros expose the inconsistency:

- tabulated Qwen2.5-1.5B SFT TPR on `prompt_injections`: `.948`;
- GPT-5.4/low TPR: `.741`;
- reported paired delta: `+.185` rather than `.948 - .741 = +.207`.

The emitter intentionally takes the table values from the mean-metric object and the delta from the
ensemble object ([`emit_frontier_general_h2h_tex.py:85-96`](../../experiments/emit_frontier_general_h2h_tex.py#L85-L96)).
The paired row bootstrap also treats the averaged score vector as fixed, so it carries no training-seed
uncertainty.

There is a second selection problem. The emitter scans all 12 represented-source SFT cells, keeps cells
whose nominal 95% CI excludes zero, and chooses the largest delta as the headline
([`emit_frontier_general_h2h_tex.py:57-75`](../../experiments/emit_frontier_general_h2h_tex.py#L57-L75)).
The printed CI is therefore post-selection and unadjusted. Reporting “5 of 12 clear zero” does not make
the selected interval valid.

**Recommendation.** Pre-specify one aggregate estimand across represented sources and checkpoints, or
pre-specify one cell independently of its result. For SFT, use one coherent target: either average
per-seed paired deltas and bootstrap both rows and seeds, or explicitly call the score average a five-model
ensemble and price its five-pass serving cost. Apply familywise/FDR control or label all per-cell intervals
exploratory. Do not retain the current `.948`, `.741`, `+.185` combination.

### C3. “Preregistered confirmatory” is not an accurate evidence label

**Severity:** critical  
**Affected dimensions:** correctness, readability

The abstract and front-page scope box call the starting-type study “preregistered confirmatory”
([`unified_report.tex:128-131`](unified_report.tex#L128-L131),
[`unified_report.tex:157-163`](unified_report.tex#L157-L163)). The study itself discloses contrary facts:
the registry is `dev_nonfinal`, no release lock exists, and the same inspected 3,308 rows are reused
([`act-adaptation.tex:4-16`](sections/act-adaptation.tex#L4-L16)). It further discloses that:

- Llama-Guard was scored with two harness bugs, so its constant output measures the instrument, not the
  model ([`act-adaptation.tex:97-107`](sections/act-adaptation.tex#L97-L107));
- the invalid cell was retained even though the preregistered eligibility rule required excluding
  nonconstant verdict likelihoods ([`act-adaptation.tex:109-121`](sections/act-adaptation.tex#L109-L121));
- the preregistered starting-type interaction `Gamma` was not computed even though the stated fallback
  condition did not occur ([`act-adaptation.tex:123-134`](sections/act-adaptation.tex#L123-L134)).

Calling the retained zero “conservative” does not repair protocol validity. A correctly rendered and
scored Llama-Guard cell might move in either direction; the report cannot infer its counterfactual result
from the broken run. Nor does showing that a five-family *point estimate* is stronger substitute for
recomputing the registered bounds after applying the eligibility rule.

**Recommendation.** In this version, rename the evidence “analysis-preregistered, data-inspected,
developmental.” Remove “confirmatory,” “confirmed,” and “bound-confirmed” from the abstract, section title,
and synthesis. For a confirmatory result: finalize and lock the registry, repair the native scoring
contract, preflight eligibility before opening outcomes, compute every registered estimand, and run on a
genuinely uninspected cohort. If the current data are retained, rerun the five-family bootstrap after the
protocol-specified exclusion and label it a sensitivity analysis.

### C4. The mortgage benchmark contradicts its sealed and family-isolated claims

**Severity:** critical  
**Affected dimensions:** correctness, novelty

The manuscript says the 95-row private test is sealed and that near-twins are assigned wholly to one
split ([`act4-mortgage.tex:57-64`](sections/act4-mortgage.tex#L57-L64)). The data card likewise says the
private test is not in the bundle ([`DATA_CARD.md:14-17`](../../mortgage-benchmark/benchmark/v1_hmda2022/DATA_CARD.md#L14-L17)).
Yet `mortgage-benchmark/benchmark/v1_hmda2022/private_test.jsonl` is a tracked, text-bearing 95-row file.
It may be named private, but it is not sealed from the repository or the researcher.

The split also contains a direct near-twin leak. `PAIR-0000#1` is a true protected-class minimal pair:
the reference arm (`MGB-BEN-00377`) is in `train`, while the wheelchair arm (`MGB-BEN-00374`) is in
`public_test`. They differ primarily in the protected-trait phrase. Their `family_id` values differ, so
the splitter—which groups only `content_family`—does not catch the pair
([`split.py:15-57`](../../mortgage-benchmark/magen/split.py#L15-L57)). The protected-pair validator checks
only that both variants exist and are G0/D0/PASS; it never requires one split/family
([`validate.py:54-67`](../../mortgage-benchmark/magen/validate.py#L54-L67)).

This leak does not affect the reported *zero-shot* base comparison, because those guards did not train on
the mortgage train split. It does invalidate the release's future train/test isolation claim and any
fine-tuned benchmark use.

**Recommendation.** Group by the transitive closure of `content_family`, `family_id`, and `pair_id`, then
regenerate all splits and rerun checksums/evaluations. Add an explicit cross-split pair gate. Stop calling
the tracked 95 rows sealed; a genuinely sealed acceptance cohort must never enter the public repository or
researcher-visible build. Treat the current 994 rows as a public development benchmark.

## Major findings

### M1. “One order of magnitude fewer misses” is a numerical error

The frontier serving caption claims “one order of magnitude fewer missed unsafe prompts”
([`frontier_serving_table.tex:3`](generated/frontier_serving_table.tex#L3)). At the reported 5% FPR,
GPT-5.4/low has TPR `.896`, so its miss rate is `.104`; the best small panel base has TPR `.787`, so its
miss rate is `.213`. That is about **2.0x fewer misses**, not 10x. Against the best 32B open base (`.830`),
it is only about **1.6x**. Correct the caption and every derivative slide or HTML edition.

### M2. The frontier “conservative” and scaling extrapolations are not established

The paper says coarse integer risk scores handicap the hosted model, so any frontier advantage is
conservative ([`unified_report.tex:342-345`](unified_report.tex#L342-L345)); the frontier table repeats
that all deltas are conservative. Coarsening creates ties, but without the latent within-tie ordering it
does not prove which direction AP or TPR would move. A finer score could improve or worsen ordering within
each tie block. State that the graded frontier signal has limited resolution; do not assign a direction.

Likewise, an 8x parameter increase from Qwen3-4B to Qwen3-32B buys about the same TPR increment as the
remaining gap, and the prose concludes that closing the gap “would take at least another order of
magnitude” ([`unified_report.tex:393-405`](unified_report.tex#L393-L405)). Two non-monotonic scale points
(8B fails to improve on 4B) do not support this extrapolation. Fit and validate a scaling model over more
points, or report only the observed ladder.

### M3. The matched-FPR results are descriptive ROC points, not deployable thresholds

The frontier and head-to-head code select each 5% FPR threshold from the negative labels of the same rows
used to report TPR ([`eval_frontier_general_h2h.py:82-95`](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L82-L95)).
This is legitimate for comparing empirical discrimination at a fixed ROC budget, but it is not a threshold
that could be deployed without label access. Several passages call the result a deployable operating point
or turn it directly into a gating recommendation. Use a separate calibration split to choose thresholds
and a sealed acceptance split to evaluate realized FPR/TPR; otherwise consistently call these
retrospective ROC operating points.

### M4. The paper's scope spine is stale after the frontier additions

The introduction says the output is an estimation-only characterization of four compact 1.5–4B
checkpoints plus one external replication ([`unified_report.tex:247-253`](unified_report.tex#L247-L253)).
The current report also includes a ten-checkpoint adaptation study, Qwen3-8B/32B scale ladder, six hosted
frontier configurations, six purpose-built guards, a selective cascade, and a general-benchmark
head-to-head. The related-work “gap” still describes only the old three-part paper
([`related-work.tex:159-168`](sections/related-work.tex#L159-L168)). Rewrite the abstract, scope,
contribution list, related-work map, and evidence ledger from one current inventory.

### M5. Closest current work is missing from the novelty comparison

The paper cites important predecessors, including GuardBench and Augmented Policy Training, but it omits
several closer comparisons available before this review date:

- [SafeRoute (ACL Findings 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.105/) directly trains a router
  that sends hard guard inputs from a small model to a larger guard. This is essential context for the
  manuscript's small-versus-frontier cascade and its claim that score-margin routing is the wrong signal.
- [Watching the AI Watchdogs (NAACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-short.22/) studies fairness
  and perturbation sensitivity of moderation classifiers, directly adjacent to the protected-class
  invariance instrument.
- [Policy Compliance of User Requests (ACL Industry 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.21/)
  introduces annotated user-request policy-compliance evaluation and compares small and large models.
- [ML-Bench&Guard (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00689) constructs regulation-grounded safety data and
  a 1.5B/7B policy-conditioned compliance guard across 14 languages.

These papers do not erase the exact paired estimand, but they narrow the novelty of routing, policy-
grounded compliance, small-guard deployment, and fairness evaluation. Add a literal comparison table with
columns for paired base-to-tune deltas, source-held-out transfer, threshold protocol, routing, policy
grounding, human label tier, and regulated-domain coverage. Replace “what none of them reports” with a
bounded claim supported by that table.

## Dimension reviews

### 1. Presentation — 2.6/5

**What works**

- The restrained maroon/blue/green palette, consistent section headers, and Evidence/Decision/Boundary
  boxes make a long technical report easier to scan.
- Figures 1, 12, 15, and the specialization planes communicate real structure rather than decoration.
- Tables generally state estimands, row counts, and caveats in captions.

**What fails**

- This is an **86-page article**, with the main narrative, appendices, and references sharing one visual
  hierarchy. It is neither a conference paper nor an efficiently navigable technical monograph.
- PDF page 2 contains only the scope box and is otherwise blank because `\clearpage` follows it
  ([`unified_report.tex:154-168`](unified_report.tex#L154-L168)). PDF page 31 similarly contains only the
  Act II takeaway box because every section invokes `\clearpage` (`unified_report.tex:37`).
- The title page fits a dense, roughly 500-word abstract almost to the footer. A reader sees exact deltas,
  four findings, evidence tiers, artifact counts, and benchmark release information before learning the
  paper's simplest question.
- Several tables use `\footnotesize` and long caption paragraphs; the 31-input result surface reads like
  an audit ledger rather than a paper.
- A clean Tectonic build succeeds and reproduces the committed PDF text, but emits overfull boxes in
  `act1.tex` and `tab_matched_fpr_gen.tex`, many severe underfull boxes in the limitations ledger, a missing
  bold-small-caps font shape, and repeatedly reports that the `.bbl` changed. It stops after six TeX passes
  with “rerun seems needed.”

**Recommendations**

1. Produce a 12–16 page main paper around one claim: paired specialization and its matched-FPR consequence.
2. Move the mortgage construction, ensembling catalog, scale ladder, full ledgers, and detailed recipes to
   appendices or companion artifacts.
3. Remove per-section forced page breaks; fix the two orphan pages and table overflow.
4. Reduce the abstract to 180–250 words with one primary estimand, one main result, one remedy, and one
   scope sentence.
5. Make a final visual QA gate fail on overfull boxes, unresolved references, or a non-converged build.

### 2. Readability — 2.7/5

**What works**

- Key distinctions—ranking versus calibration, represented versus transfer, expert versus LLM-judge
  labels, retrospective versus preregistered—are explained in plain language.
- The paper usually tells the reader what a result does *not* license.
- The negative protected-pair result is explained honestly rather than hidden.

**What fails**

- The same epistemic caveats recur in the abstract, front-page box, Act boxes, synthesis table,
  reproducibility section, conclusion, and Appendix E. Repetition adds length without resolving the
  inconsistent “confirmatory” label.
- Act numbering is difficult to follow: the adaptation study is an “Interlude (Act I, confirmed),” the
  file called `act3.tex` is Act II, domains are Act III, and the frontier section expands Act III without
  appearing in the original three-question roadmap.
- The manuscript alternates among paper, report, workflow, benchmark release, and deployment guide. The
  reader cannot tell which contribution is primary.
- The title foregrounds “High-Compliance Regulated Domains,” while the strongest results are general-
  safety retrospective experiments and the mortgage labels are explicitly not SME-adjudicated.
- The prose often stacks qualification inside qualification. Exact caveats are valuable, but they should
  live once in a claim/evidence table and be referenced thereafter.

**Recommendations**

1. Choose one genre and one primary claim. My recommendation is an empirical measurement paper, not a
   deployment guide or compliance paper.
2. Use a one-page claim ledger near the start: claim, estimand, data, label tier, evidence status, and
   allowed conclusion.
3. Rename sections by research question rather than “Act” labels, and make the roadmap match the current
   evidence inventory.
4. Keep each caveat at the first claim and in the ledger; remove repeated defensive prose.
5. Scope the title to “small safety-guard specialization and transfer” unless the mortgage benchmark is
   independently validated.

### 3. Novelty — 2.8/5

**What is genuinely useful**

- The paired same-checkpoint estimand, with explicit represented-source versus source-held-out transfer,
  is a good measurement discipline even though each ingredient is standard.
- Testing calibrated base+adapter output averaging as a targeted repair, including an equal-cost SFT+SFT
  control, is a useful guard-specific empirical contribution.
- Connecting ranking, matched alarm budgets, label tiers, and regulated-domain probes in one evidence
  ledger is valuable systems work.

**Why the score is below 3**

- The paper explicitly introduces no new model, metric, or training algorithm
  ([`related-work.tex:4-12`](sections/related-work.tex#L4-L12)). Its main novelty is packaging and
  experimental discipline.
- Fine-tuning degradation, policy overfitting, ID/OOD guard evaluation, calibrated ensembling, domain-
  specific policy evaluation, fairness audits, and small/large guard routing all have direct prior art.
- The frontier section is a benchmark comparison, not a new method, and its strongest cell is selected
  retrospectively.
- The mortgage benchmark's distinctive dual-label claim is weakened by nested labels, no SME gold, no
  D=1 protected pairs, a public “private” test, and pair leakage.
- Missing closest work makes the “what is missing” claim too broad.

**Recommendations**

1. Claim novelty at the level of the *joint protocol*, not the individual components.
2. Add the closest-work comparison table described in M5 and a documented literature cutoff/search.
3. Turn the composition result into a stronger contribution by comparing output averaging with a real
   WiSE-FT control and a pre-specified transfer objective on fresh data.
4. Treat the current frontier-distillation plan in `proposal.md` as proposed/unrun future work, never as
   evidence that the present SLM closes the frontier gap.

### 4. Correctness — 1.9/5

**Claims that appear supportable after correction**

- Ordinary SFT raises represented-source AP on the fixed four-checkpoint panel.
- Transfer effects are heterogeneous and can be negative at a matched false-alarm budget.
- Base+SFT output averaging descriptively improves transfer relative to SFT on this fixed panel, at an
  extra inference pass and without a demonstrated transferable threshold.
- On ExpGuard, GPT-5.4/low has higher TPR@5%FPR than the evaluated small panel; the small model retains a
  large latency/cost advantage under the measured but non-equivalent serving setups.
- The current mortgage public split is useful as a synthetic development probe, not a compliance audit.

**Claims that are not currently supportable**

- The reproduction pipeline byte-checks the advertised evidence surface.
- The selected represented-source SFT cell beats GPT by the displayed paired estimate and CI.
- The adaptation study is confirmatory.
- The mortgage private test is sealed or every near-twin is split-isolated.
- The frontier model yields an order-of-magnitude reduction in misses.
- Coarse hosted scores make all frontier deltas conservative.
- The observed 4B→32B ladder implies at least another order of magnitude is required.

**Recommendations**

Resolve C1–C4 before interpreting any further statistics. Then regenerate every claim from one manifest,
run arithmetic consistency checks on all displayed `a`, `b`, and `a-b` triples, and separate exploratory
selection from preregistered tests. A fresh independent gold cohort is required for confirmation; the
inspected ExpGuard and Paper A rows should remain diagnostic evidence only.

## Review limits

This review did not retrain models, access gated ExpGuard prompt text, re-annotate mortgage labels, or
claim legal/compliance authority. Four Act I tables require the paper's lock-pinned environment and were
not re-derived here; this is reported as unverified, not treated as failure of their numerical content.

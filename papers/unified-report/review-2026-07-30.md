## Executive verdict

This revision is materially better than the version reviewed at <code>158bc30</code>. It fixes the
five-seed estimator mismatch, corrects the 10× miss-rate error, withdraws unsupported directional and
scaling claims, replaces “confirmatory” with “analysis-preregistered” in many prominent locations,
discloses the spent mortgage holdout and split pair leak, and makes the reproduction checker complete
enough to report rather than crash.

Those repairs do not yet make the paper correct. The most important remaining problem is not cosmetic:
the registered adaptation hypotheses were defined over the purpose-built panel, while the analyzer
computes their H statistics over all general and purpose-built checkpoints, including a Qwen family
that mixes both starting types. The published bounds therefore estimate a different quantity from the
registered RQ1/RQ2 estimand.

The replacement frontier headline also cannot be treated as pre-specified or confirmatory. It was
introduced after the outcomes were inspected and after the selected-cell headline failed multiplicity.
Its point estimate is arithmetically coherent, but its interval does not preserve the crossed
seed/source structure, and the advertised “Holm-adjusted interval” is not produced by a valid Holm
procedure. The per-row GPT inputs needed to recompute it are gitignored, unlocked, and absent from a
clean checkout; both H2H TeX outputs are outside the reproduction harness.

The paper should therefore be read as a large, unusually candid **retrospective fixed-panel technical
report**, not yet as a confirmatory or fully reproducible research paper.

## Submission-blocking findings

### C1. The reported adaptation bounds do not test the registered purpose-built-panel hypotheses

**Severity:** critical

**Affected dimensions:** correctness, readability

The preregistered proposal defines RQ1 over the “entire preregistered complete purpose-built panel” and
defines RQ2 over “the same equal-family panel”
([<code>proposal.md:126-154</code>](proposal.md#L126)). The normative primary contract likewise keeps
general and purpose-built panels separate and defines PC1 around the purpose-built checkpoints
([<code>primary_contract.json:57-88</code>](../../artifacts/starting_type_adaptation_v1/protocol/primary_contract.json#L57),
[<code>133-143</code>](../../artifacts/starting_type_adaptation_v1/protocol/primary_contract.json#L133)).
The implementation does something else:

- [<code>analyze_starting_type_adaptation.py:171-204</code>](../../experiments/analyze_starting_type_adaptation.py#L171)
  builds a panel from every checkpoint without filtering by <code>starting_type</code>.
- [<code>analyze_starting_type_adaptation.py:258-305</code>](../../experiments/analyze_starting_type_adaptation.py#L258)
  groups all checkpoints by model family and computes H over all resulting families.
- The single Qwen family combines general Qwen checkpoints with Qwen3Guard purpose-built checkpoints.

Consequently, the displayed H_gain, H_conc, H_preserve, and H_cost bounds are six-family mixed-panel
statistics, not the registered purpose-built-panel estimands. This is not repaired by calling the study
“analysis-preregistered,” and it is not repaired by the exact 6/5 rescaling after removing the Llama
null family: that rescaling still applies to the wrong family set.

The paper itself now admits that all ten preflight artifacts say <code>eligible:false</code>, the
training-dependent eligibility checks were skipped, the invalid Llama cell was retained against the
rule, and the registered Γ interaction was not computed
([<code>act-adaptation.tex:97-147</code>](sections/act-adaptation.tex#L97)). Despite this, it still says
RQ1 is “supported” and “bound-confirmed”
([<code>act-adaptation.tex:61-71</code>](sections/act-adaptation.tex#L61),
[<code>150-156</code>](sections/act-adaptation.tex#L150)).

**Recommendation.** Remove the RQ1/RQ2 support verdicts from this paper. Recompute purpose-built-only
and general-only summaries plus the registered Γ as explicitly post-hoc sensitivities, after enforcing
the eligibility rule. For a confirmatory claim, finalize and hash the registry, run every preflight
before outcomes exist, use a clean valid panel, preserve starting type in the analysis schema, and score
a genuinely uninspected cohort.

### C2. The replacement H2H headline is post hoc, not pre-specified

**Severity:** critical

**Affected dimensions:** correctness, novelty

The paper now promotes an equal-source/equal-checkpoint aggregate of ΔTPR = +0.083
[+0.008,+0.163] and ΔAP = +0.039 [+0.014,+0.072] as the “claim-bearing quantity”
([<code>unified_report.tex:569-592</code>](unified_report.tex#L569)). The aggregate was absent from
<code>158bc30</code> and was added in <code>bd3fadc</code> only after the prior maximum-of-12 headline
failed multiplicity. The code and artifact nevertheless call it “pre-specified”
([<code>eval_frontier_general_h2h.py:341-348</code>](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L341),
[<code>435-445</code>](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L435)).

The point estimate itself checks out: the three represented-source means are approximately +0.1574,
+0.0280, and +0.0646, whose equal-source mean is +0.0833. The defect is evidential, not arithmetic.
After seeing the results, many plausible summaries could have been chosen: equal rows, equal sources,
equal checkpoints, only tuned checkpoints, all checkpoints, or a regime interaction. Selecting a new
summary after the old headline fails does not make it pre-specified merely because the final rule no
longer selects the largest cell.

**Recommendation.** Relabel the aggregate as a post-hoc descriptive fixed-panel summary and remove
“claim-bearing”/“pre-specified.” Show all reasonable weighting sensitivities. Freeze one primary
aggregate before collecting a fresh independent cohort; only that prospective result can carry the
frontier-beating claim.

### C3. The multiplicity and aggregate uncertainty calculations are not statistically coherent

**Severity:** critical

**Affected dimensions:** correctness

The individual-cell repair is useful: the point estimate is now the mean of per-seed paired differences,
so it equals the printed guard minus reference values. The new familywise procedure is not a valid Holm
procedure, however:

1. It ranks cells by |Δ| divided by half the nominal percentile-CI width rather than by valid p-values.
2. It assigns a rank-dependent alpha.
3. It rescales asymmetric percentile-bootstrap bounds using normal critical-value ratios.
4. It does not implement Holm’s step-down stopping or adjusted p-values.

The relevant implementation is
[<code>eval_frontier_general_h2h.py:355-385</code>](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L355).
The displayed [−0.033,+0.475] may be a conservative normal-approximation sensitivity, but it is not a
Holm-adjusted bootstrap confidence interval. Therefore “0 of 12 after familywise correction” has not
been established by the implemented procedure, even if that qualitative outcome may survive a correct
analysis.

The aggregate bootstrap has a separate structural problem
([<code>eval_frontier_general_h2h.py:387-430</code>](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L387)):

- It iterates over the same three sources in every replicate; sources are never resampled. The interval
  is conditional on exactly those sources.
- A trained adapter seed is scored on every source, but the implementation redraws seed indices
  independently inside each source/checkpoint loop. The same seed identity should be sampled jointly
  across sources for a checkpoint to preserve covariance.
- It samples rows rather than the available <code>family_id</code> clusters, unlike the main paper’s
  family-aware uncertainty protocol.

These choices can narrow or widen the interval unpredictably; the +0.008 lower bound should not be
treated as reliable until the crossed structure is recomputed.

**Recommendation.** Retain bootstrap draws, compute valid two-sided p-values for all 12 fixed
hypotheses, and apply an actual Holm step-down adjustment. Use a simultaneous/max-T or explicitly
Bonferroni interval if adjusted intervals are required. For the aggregate, resample evaluation
families, preserve one adapter-seed draw across all sources for each checkpoint, and state whether
sources are fixed or sampled. With only three purposively chosen represented sources, any
source-population conclusion should remain descriptive.

### C5. The evaluated cascade and the recommended router are different methods

**Severity:** critical

**Affected dimensions:** correctness, novelty, readability

The actual cascade escalates rows nearest the local guard’s decision line, measured by rank distance
([<code>emit_cascade_tex.py:68-86</code>](../../experiments/emit_cascade_tex.py#L68)). That is a
score-confidence/margin-family router. The paper describes this result at
[<code>unified_report.tex:623-643</code>](unified_report.tex#L623), then repeatedly recommends
escalating on “unfamiliarity rather than on score margin”
([<code>unified_report.tex:674-683</code>](unified_report.tex#L674),
[<code>757-766</code>](unified_report.tex#L757)).

No familiarity score, detector, training objective, threshold, or evaluation appears in the repository.
The tested cascade therefore cannot be evidence for the proposed unfamiliarity router. Its local
decision line and final 5% FPR threshold are also selected from labels on the same ExpGuard rows used
for evaluation, so the curve is retrospective and optimistically tuned, not an end-to-end deployment
estimate.

**Recommendation.** Call unfamiliarity routing an untested hypothesis. On development data, implement
a source/familiarity detector and compare it with rank-distance, raw margin, entropy/calibrated error,
random routing, and a SafeRoute-style learned router at identical hosted-call budgets. Freeze the router
and global threshold, then evaluate once on a fresh acceptance set.

### M2. Matched-FPR values remain retrospective ROC points

The H2H and frontier functions choose the 5% threshold from the evaluated negatives and compute TPR on
those same rows
([<code>eval_frontier_general_h2h.py:82-94</code>](../../experiments/eval_frontier_general_h2h.py#L82)).
This is acceptable for a descriptive ROC comparison, including bootstrap re-estimation of that
functional. It is not a threshold available to a production system without labels.

**Recommendation.** Keep “TPR at an empirical matched-FPR ROC point” for the current result. For
operational claims, select the threshold on a disjoint calibration set, freeze it, and report realized
FPR/TPR on an untouched acceptance set.

### M3. The closest current literature remains missing

The exact paired estimator remains useful, but several adjacent contributions are already established:

- [SafeRoute (ACL Findings 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.105/) directly learns
  small-to-large guard routing and compares uncertainty baselines.
- [Watching the AI Watchdogs (NAACL 2025)](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-short.22/) audits
  moderation classifiers for fairness and perturbation robustness.
- [Policy Compliance of User Requests (ACL Industry 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.21/)
  benchmarks annotated natural-language requests against explicit policies.
- [ML-Bench&Guard (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00689) derives regulation-grounded multilingual
  data and trains 1.5B/7B policy-conditioned guards.
- [COMPASS (ACL 2026)](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.2139/) evaluates organization-specific
  allow/deny policies across eight industry scenarios.
- [When Safety Geometry Collapses (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02914) directly studies ordinary
  domain fine-tuning of LlamaGuard, WildGuard, and Granite Guardian.

These do not erase the same-checkpoint represented/transfer estimand. They do narrow claims around
routing, policy grounding, fairness instrumentation, and the novelty of adapting released guards.

**Recommendation.** Add a closest-work table with columns for paired base-to-tune change,
source-held-out transfer, matched-FPR protocol, routing method, policy grounding, label tier, fairness
construct, and regulated-domain coverage. Replace “what none of them reports” with:

> A same-checkpoint paired measurement of LoRA-induced change across represented and source-held-out
> regimes, together with a calibrated base-plus-adapter composition test under matched false-alarm
> budgets.
